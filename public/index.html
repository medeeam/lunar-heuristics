    An exploratory prototype by Lunar Heuristics for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. Not a flight-planning product.

    Real ephemerides, explicit assumptions

    Sun (10) and Earth (399) positions come from NASA/JPL Horizons, observed from custom coordinates on the Moon (301). We request airless apparent azimuth and elevation of each body’s center, every hour, at 0 km above the lunar reference sphere. Horizons supplies lunar orientation and libration effects.

    Azimuth runs clockwise from north. Visibility means the body’s center is above the selected clearance. Crossings are linearly interpolated between samples; short events may be missed. Earth visibility is a geometric opportunity, not a radio link budget or ground-station guarantee.

    Terrain and solar-power limitations

    No LOLA elevation model is included yet. Craters, local slopes, horizon dip, terrain shadowing, and finite body disks are not modeled. The clearance slider is a uniform sensitivity test, not terrain data. Solar factor is max(0, sin(solar elevation)) for a horizontal panel, set to zero below clearance. It is not electrical output; efficiency, temperature, eclipses, and array orientation need separate modeling.

    Sources, reproducibility & next steps

    The bundled demo contains actual JPL responses for 15 November–15 December 2026. Other dates and custom locations use our server-side JPL data service. Raw query parameters and source responses are retained in the downloaded provenance file.
